## Prepare Specific Examples

Behavioral questions ask how you work with others and respond to uncertainty. Prepare examples about learning a tool, resolving disagreement, asking for help, receiving review feedback, and recovering from a mistake.

Organize each example around the situation, your responsibility, your actions, and the outcome. Describe what you personally did and what changed. If the result was imperfect, explain what you learned and what you would do differently.

Avoid memorizing a speech that does not answer the question. Listen, choose a relevant example, and provide enough detail for follow-up questions. If you have no workplace example, a course project, volunteer contribution, or personal project can still show real behavior.

Prepare questions that help you assess the team: How are junior developers onboarded? What does review feedback look like? How are incidents handled? How do developers get help when a task is unclear? Ask for examples rather than only general promises.

## Final Practice

Rehearse one technical walkthrough, one coding problem, and two behavioral examples with a peer. **Check:** ask them what was unclear, revise the explanation, and keep the facts accurate. After an interview, record the questions that exposed gaps and choose a concrete practice task for each.
