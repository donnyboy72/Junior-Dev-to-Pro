## Tell a Technical Story with Evidence

Prepare a short explanation of one project: who it serves, the problem it solves, your contribution, how the system fits together, and what you learned. Then be ready to go deeper into a specific decision.

For this learning site, a concise explanation could cover Markdown lessons, a shared Jekyll layout, automatic navigation, and static publication. A useful tradeoff is that static pages simplify hosting, while interactive challenges still need browser-side logic and deliberate state handling.

Choose a change you actually made. Explain the starting problem, alternatives considered, resulting behavior, and verification. If you do not have production traffic, use honest evidence such as checked links and reproducible examples rather than inventing usage statistics.

Be clear about individual and team contributions. Discuss a limitation and how you would investigate an improvement. Knowing the boundaries of your project is more convincing than claiming expertise in every component.

## Practice

Give a two-minute walkthrough and draw the path from a source lesson to a reader’s browser. **Check:** name one design decision, one concrete check, and one limitation you can explain without reading a script.
